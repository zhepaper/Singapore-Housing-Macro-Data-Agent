# Singapore Private Non-Landed Residential Market: Indicator Analysis

**Information cut-off:** 2026-10-01

## Introduction

This report asks what the latest public data suggest about Singapore's private non-landed residential sale and rental markets. It examines the two markets separately and considers whether recent changes in demand, financing, vacancy and prospective supply are consistent with the observed movements in prices and rents.

## Data and Analytical Approach

The official URA price and rental indices show how the two markets moved. These outcomes are considered alongside economic growth, borrowing conditions, non-resident demand, vacancy and the residential pipeline. Most observations are quarterly. Monthly SORA is represented by its value at the end of each quarter so that it can be compared with the housing indices, while annual population data remain annual rather than being converted into artificial quarterly estimates. The results describe how the indicators moved together; they do not establish causal effects or constitute a forecast.

## Rental-Market Findings

In 2026 Q2, the private non-landed rental index was 162.5, with its level in the first quarter of 2009 set to 100. Compared with the previous quarter, the private non-landed rental index was 0.4% higher in 2026 Q2. Compared with a year earlier, the private non-landed rental index was 1.8% higher in 2026 Q2. The outcome evidence shows that rents continued to increase over both the latest quarter and the year, although the quarterly movement was considerably more limited than the annual comparison.

Non-resident employment increased by 9,200 in 2026 Q2. The non-resident population was 1,906,660 in 2025. Compared with a year earlier, the non-resident population was 2.7% higher in 2025. These demand-side measures are consistent with continued growth in the potential tenant base. This interpretation accords with [Chia, W.-M., Li, M. and Tang, Y. (2017)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jhouse/v36y2017icp44-61.html), [Ji, P. I. and Bhandari, S. B. (2022)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/ijhmap/ijhma-02-2021-0023.html), [DiPasquale, D. and Wheaton, W. C. (1992)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1540-6229.00579) and [Urban Redevelopment Authority and Housing & Development Board (2026)](https://www.ura.gov.sg/news/media/pr26-03/), but the measures should still be treated as proxies: employment and population data do not identify whether the individuals concerned occupied private non-landed homes.

In 2026 Q2, the private non-landed vacancy rate was 5.4%. Compared with the previous quarter, the private non-landed vacancy rate was 0.02 percentage points higher in 2026 Q2. Compared with a year earlier, the private non-landed vacancy rate was 1.49 percentage points lower in 2026 Q2. Vacancy was almost unchanged over the latest quarter but remained appreciably below its year-earlier level. That combination is consistent with continued utilisation of the available stock and provides a plausible market-tightness mechanism for positive rental growth. It does not establish that the demand indicators caused the rental movement.

Taken together, the rental evidence indicates a firm market rather than a renewed sharp acceleration. Positive rent changes, expanding potential tenant demand and lower vacancy relative to the previous year point in the same broad direction, while the small quarterly changes warrant a measured interpretation.

## Sale-Market Findings

In 2026 Q2, the private non-landed price index was 210.6, with its level in the first quarter of 2009 set to 100. Compared with the previous quarter, the private non-landed price index was 0.1% lower in 2026 Q2. Compared with a year earlier, the private non-landed price index was 1.8% higher in 2026 Q2. The price outcome therefore combines a marginal quarterly decline with positive annual growth. This is more consistent with a loss of short-term momentum than with a broad annual contraction.

Real GDP growth was 5.9% year on year in 2026 Q2. Real GDP increased by 1.4% quarter on quarter on a seasonally adjusted basis in 2026 Q2. Economic activity was positive both relative to a year earlier and relative to the immediately preceding quarter. The potential income and confidence channel is consistent with [Tu, Y. (2004)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1080/0042098042000178708), [International Monetary Fund (2018)](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2018/cr18245.pdf) and [Rangel, G. J. and Ng, J. W. J. (2017)](https://www.mfa.com.my/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/vol252_pp.15-31-Rangel-Ng.pdf), but the descriptive evidence cannot isolate it from other influences.

At the end of 2026 Q2, three-month Compounded SORA was 1.08% per annum. Compared with the previous quarter, three-month Compounded SORA was 1.17 basis points higher in 2026 Q2. The financing evidence shows that the approved quarter-end rate did not fall over the latest quarter. It therefore provided little additional easing in borrowing conditions during the period, although the movement itself was small and should not be treated as a standalone explanation of prices.

In 2026 Q2, the private non-landed residential pipeline was 51,677 dwelling units. Compared with the previous quarter, the private non-landed residential pipeline was 3,566 dwelling units higher in 2026 Q2. Compared with a year earlier, the private non-landed residential pipeline was 6.6% higher in 2026 Q2. The prospective supply pipeline increased over both the quarter and the year. Pipeline units are not completed homes, so their immediate effect on available supply cannot be inferred from these figures. Even so, the increase may moderate expectations of future scarcity as projects progress toward completion.

Overall, sale-market conditions were less firm at the quarterly horizon than rental-market conditions. Economic activity remained supportive, but financing did not provide further easing and prospective supply expanded, which is consistent with prices remaining above their year-earlier level while changing little in the latest quarter.

## Limitations

The selected indicators are theoretically relevant but do not identify causal effects. They are also released at different frequencies and with different publication lags. Non-resident employment and population measure potential rental demand rather than actual private tenancy, and the residential pipeline measures prospective rather than completed supply. The analysis does not control for policy changes, location, property characteristics or compositional shifts within the private non-landed segment. Results are therefore best read as a disciplined monitoring assessment bounded by the stated information cut-off.

## Conclusion

The latest evidence indicates that the private non-landed rental market remained firmer than the sale market. Positive rental growth coincided with expanding demand-side indicators and vacancy below its year-earlier level, while sale prices retained positive annual growth but showed little quarterly momentum. Macroeconomic activity remained favourable, but financing conditions and a larger prospective supply pipeline provided countervailing influences. These relationships are informative for monitoring market conditions, but they do not constitute a formal forecast.
